“We don’t sell furniture. We sell the fact that you will not have to buy this chair again for thirty years.” — Ada Keller, founder & lead joiner

It started with a broken dresser. In 1987, Ada Keller carried a splintered Victorian dresser into the back room of a rented workshop and refused to throw it out. The repair took four evenings. A neighbour came in for glue and stayed for a conversation that became the founding principle of the showroom: things should be worth repairing.

Thirty-eight years later, the workshop has grown into a gallery, but the rule hasn’t moved. Every piece in the building is designed to be opened up, sanded, re-upholstered and re-oiled. We keep the original joinery drawings for everything we’ve made, and our repair bench has been occupied, without interruption, since 1989.

We buy our wood from three family-run mills within a day’s drive, our wool from a single weaver in the Alentejo, and our leather from a tannery that still uses chestnut bark. It makes us slower. We have chosen to be slower.
