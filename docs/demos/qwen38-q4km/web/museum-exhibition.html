Artist Statement · 01

On the work

“I am not interested in the light itself. I paint the moment it gives up — the half-second before a room stops being bright, when everything you own is briefly, unfairly, yours.”

Over five years, Mirela Anand has painted from the same unglazed window in her Rotterdam studio, usually at dusk, usually alone. Half-Light gathers thirty-two of the resulting canvases — oils, encaustics, and pigment on linen — into a single slow procession. The paintings are hung in order of the clock, not of date: the room begins at 16:41 and does not let you leave until 19:03.

Anand works in what she calls "second light" — the residual glow a room keeps after the sun has passed. Her brushwork strips the scene to a horizon, a sliver, a residue of colour. What remains feels less like a landscape than a memory of one, or the exact tone of a song you can no longer name.

The exhibition is the first full survey of the series and includes eight works never shown publicly, along with the studio notebooks in which Anand recorded the weather, the temperature, and the time of day for each painting.

Mirela Anand Rotterdam, 1976 —
